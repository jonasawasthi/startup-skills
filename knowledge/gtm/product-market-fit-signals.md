# Product-market fit signals

## Attributed

- **The Ellis question.** Users are asked how they would feel if they could no longer use the product: *very disappointed*, *somewhat disappointed* or *not disappointed*. As Vohra reports it, Ellis benchmarked nearly 100 startups and found that those with fewer than about 40% "very disappointed" users usually struggled to grow.
- **Superhuman's engine (Vohra, First Round Review, 2018).** Survey only users who have recently used the product (Superhuman surveyed people who had used it at least twice in the previous two weeks). About 40 responses gives a directional result. Then:
  1. segment the responses to find the high-expectation customers who would be very disappointed;
  2. learn what those users love, and what holds back the "somewhat disappointed" users who value the same core benefit;
  3. split the roadmap between doubling down on what users love and removing those blockers;
  4. repeat, and track the score as the top metric.
- Vohra reports the score rising from 22% to 33% after segmentation alone, and to 58% within three quarters. He warns it may fall as less forgiving mainstream users arrive.

## How the GTM skill applies this (original synthesis)

- **Gate paid scaling on retention evidence.** Paid acquisition, affiliates and referral incentives are scaling channels. Until there is evidence of retention or a PMF signal, the skill keeps them as small experiments with kill criteria.
- **The survey is a measurement, not a verdict.** Below about 40 responses, or when respondents are friends and family, report the result as *directional*. Combine it with retention cohorts and repeat purchases.
- **Transactional and education products**: use the repeat purchase rate, course completion and the share of buyers who return within a set window, alongside the survey.
- **Pre-launch**: there is no PMF signal yet. The plan says so and sets the first measurement point (for example, survey after the second use, and cohort retention at day 30).
