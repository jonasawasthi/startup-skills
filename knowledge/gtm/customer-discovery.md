# Customer discovery

## Attributed

- **Customer development (Blank).** Blank defines a startup as an organisation formed to *search* for a repeatable and scalable business model. Discovery comes before execution: test hypotheses about customers, problems and channels outside the building, then validate them with real customer behaviour.
- **The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick).** Its subtitle is the point: people will tell you your idea is good to be polite. Ask about their past behaviour and current spending, not their opinion of your idea or what they would do in the future.
- **Jobs to be done (Christensen et al., HBR, 2016).** Customers "hire" a product to make progress in a particular circumstance. A job has functional, social and emotional dimensions, and the circumstance matters more than the customer's demographics.
- **Buyer personas (HubSpot).** A persona is a semi-fictional profile built from research (interviews, surveys, analytics), not from imagination. B2B work separates the user from the buyer and from the people who influence the purchase.
- **Ideal customer (YC partners, 2022).** The discussion weighs the pros and cons of startups selling to other startups: early adopters buy fast but may not represent the market you want long term.

## How the GTM skill applies this (original synthesis)

1. **Roles are separate.** For every segment, record the *user*, *buyer*, *influencer* and *beneficiary*. In consumer products they are often one person. In education, the learner may use the product while a parent pays. In B2B, the buyer is rarely the user.
2. **An ICP is a hypothesis until behaviour confirms it.** Each ICP attribute carries an evidence ID or the label *hypothesis*. Interview quotes are `interview` evidence with status `reported`. Behaviour (payments, retention, repeat use) can be `verified` from data.
3. **Early adopters** have the problem now, know they have it, have tried to solve it (a workaround or spending) and can buy without a long process. List the observable signals that identify them.
4. **Willingness to pay** is ranked by strength: completed payment > pre-order or deposit > signed LOI > stated price in a structured interview > survey answer. Surveys alone never make a price "researched".
5. **No research yet?** Then the output is a research plan: 15–25 problem interviews per segment, a Mom-Test-style script, a recruitment source, and the decision each interview round informs. The skill never writes personas as if they were validated.

## Interview essentials

- Ask about the last time the problem happened, what they did, what it cost, and what they tried.
- Avoid pitching during discovery. Record exact words, because they become messaging.
- End by asking for a commitment (time, a pilot, a deposit or an introduction). Commitments are evidence; compliments are not.
