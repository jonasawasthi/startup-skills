# Growth loops

## Attributed

- **Loops, not funnels (Reforge, 2018).** The authors argue that the AARRR funnel creates strategic silos (product, acquisition and monetisation planned separately), functional silos (each team optimising its own stage, sometimes at others' expense) and a one-way flow that needs ever more input at the top. In a growth loop, the output of one cycle becomes the input of the next. They illustrate this with Pinterest: users save content, the content is distributed to search engines, and searchers arrive and save more content. Their advice is to ask how one cohort of users produces the next. Most products have many weak loops; the fastest growers depend on one or two strong ones.
- **Flywheel (HubSpot).** HubSpot presents a flywheel in place of the funnel, where customers who have a good experience drive further growth through referrals and repeat business.

## How the GTM skill applies this (original synthesis)

1. **Use AARRR for measurement** (where users drop off) **and loops for strategy** (what compounds). They answer different questions.
2. **Write each loop as a sentence**: *"A [actor] does [action], which produces [output], which reaches [new person] through [channel], who then [action]."* If any step is a hope, not an observed behaviour, label it a hypothesis.
3. **Typical loops by model:**
   - creator platforms: a creator shares a paid link with their audience; buyers discover other creators; some buyers become creators;
   - education: a learner completes a course and shares a certificate or result; peers enrol;
   - marketplaces: supply quality brings demand, demand income keeps supply active;
   - developer tools: a developer integrates the tool, and teammates and dependants see it;
   - B2B SaaS: an invited collaborator becomes a new account.
4. **Measure a loop with three numbers**: the input volume, the conversion at each step, and the cycle time. Without those numbers, a loop is a diagram, not evidence.
