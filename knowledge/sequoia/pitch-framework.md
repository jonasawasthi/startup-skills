# Sequoia's pitch framework

Sequoia's public guidance lists ten sections for presenting a company. The one-line prompts below are paraphrased.

| # | Section | Sequoia's prompt (paraphrased) | Deck slide type in Startup Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Company purpose | Define the company in a single declarative sentence | `cover` subtitle / `statement` |
| 2 | Problem | Describe the customer's pain | `points` |
| 3 | Solution | Explain your eureka moment | `split` / `points` |
| 4 | Why now? | The best companies almost always have a clear "why now" | `points` / `bullets` |
| 5 | Market potential | Identify your customer and your market | `market` |
| 6 | Competition / alternatives | Direct and indirect competitors | `comparison` / `positioning` |
| 7 | Business model | How you intend to thrive | `chart` / `bullets` |
| 8 | Team | The story of the founders and key team | `team` |
| 9 | Financials | Include them if you have any | `chart` / `table` |
| 10 | Vision | What you'll have built in five years if all goes well | `closing` |

## How it differs from the YC seed template

Sequoia puts **purpose, problem and why now** first. That sequence builds a market thesis before showing the product. YC's seed template (Harris) gets to traction quickly. The pitch-deck maker picks between them by stage and by evidence: strong traction → lead with traction (YC order); a strong market thesis but little traction → lead with purpose and why now (Sequoia order).

## Limitations

This is Sequoia's *public* guidance. It does not describe Sequoia's internal investment decision process, and the skills must never imply that following it improves odds with Sequoia.
