# Investor storytelling

## The spine of every deck

An investor deck answers one question: **why will this become a large company, and why these founders, now?** Every slide either advances that argument or supports it with evidence. If a slide does neither, cut it.

1. **Clarity first.** The cover subtitle says what the company does in plain words (Seibel's question 1; Sequoia's "single declarative sentence").
2. **One idea per slide, expressed as an action title.** "Cooperatives run on paper, so farmers wait weeks to get paid", not "Problem".
3. **Insight is the hinge.** Between problem and solution, name what the founders understand that others don't (YC "unique insight"; Sequoia "eureka").
4. **Evidence beats adjectives.** Every number has a source, and the status of every claim is visible. Investors discount unlabelled projections anyway, so labelling them builds credibility.
5. **Why now** explains why this wasn't possible or attractive five years ago.
6. **The ask is a plan:** the amount → milestones → what the next round looks like.

## Narrative patterns by evidence profile

| Profile | Lead with | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Strong traction | The traction chart on slide 2–3 | YC: title → problem → solution → traction → insight → model → market → team → ask |
| Strong market thesis, little traction | Purpose and why now | Sequoia: purpose → problem → why now → solution → market → competition → model → team → vision |
| Deep tech or long build | The insight and the technical milestone path | purpose → problem → insight → technology → milestones de-risked → market → team → ask |
| Multi-product ecosystem | One wedge product, then the ecosystem as expansion | problem → wedge → traction on wedge → ecosystem map (statuses visible) → model → ask |

## Anti-patterns the deck maker blocks

- Planned products presented as live (status pills are mandatory).
- Top-down "1% of a huge market" sizing.
- Charts without sources, and cumulative charts presented as growth.
- Advisor-heavy team slides at seed (Harris: "Nobody cares about your advisors").
- Logos of "partners" without agreements in the ledger.
