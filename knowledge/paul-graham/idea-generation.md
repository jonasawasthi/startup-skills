# Idea generation and screening

## Screening questions (each must be answered with evidence or marked unknown)

1. **Who wants this urgently, and how do we know?** Name a person or role. Cite behaviour, not opinion. (Graham: narrow and deep beats broad and shallow.)
2. **Is it noticed or invented?** Did the idea come from experiencing the problem, or from looking for an idea? Invented ideas need stronger external evidence.
3. **What changed recently?** Technology, regulation, behaviour or cost (Friedman's recipe 4). No change means asking why incumbents haven't solved it.
4. **Is there a schlep others avoid?** Operational, regulatory or integration work that keeps competitors out (Graham, *Schlep Blindness*).
5. **Is there a proxy success elsewhere?** A similar company in another geography or segment (Friedman). Note that a proxy validates demand, not execution in the new context.
6. **What's the insight?** One sentence that would surprise an informed outsider.
7. **Founder–market fit:** what can these founders do or reach that others can't?

## Red flags

- "Everyone needs this" with no specific first user.
- A solution looking for a problem ("Uber for X").
- A market defined by top-down percentages ("if we get 1% of…").
- Demand evidence that is entirely compliments or survey intent.
- A sitcom idea: plausible-sounding, and no interviewee has acted on the problem.
