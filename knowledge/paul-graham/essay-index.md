# Paul Graham: startup essay index

Original summaries for orientation and retrieval. **Read the essays themselves** at the links. Nothing here replaces them, and no essay text is reproduced.

Legend for *Skills*: EV = startup-idea-evaluator, PD = pitch-deck-maker, PG = proposal generator, RS = startup-research, FR = fundraising-strategist.

---

### How to Get Startup Ideas (Nov 2012)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
- **Thesis:** The best ideas are noticed, not invented. They come from problems the founders themselves have, often at the leading edge of a changing field ("live in the future, then build what's missing").
- **Principles:** Prefer ideas where a small number of people want something urgently over ideas many people mildly like (the "well" metaphor: narrow and deep). Beware "made-up" or "sitcom" ideas that sound plausible but that nobody urgently wants. Unsexy and schlep-heavy problems are under-explored.
- **Implications:** Ask *who wants this so much that they'd use a crappy v1 built by a startup they've never heard of?*
- **Applies when:** Generating or screening early ideas.
- **Limitations:** "Founder as user" can mislead in B2B and emerging markets, where the founder is far from the user. Substitute direct user evidence.
- **Skills:** EV, RS

### Do Things That Don't Scale (Jul 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html
- **Thesis:** Startups take off because founders make them take off. The early unscalable effort is the crank that starts the engine.
- **Principles:** Recruit users manually. Delight early users with "insanely great" attention. Start with a narrow market you can dominate. Consider consulting-like work for the first customers, and manual work behind a software facade.
- **Implications:** Validation plans and GTM should include deliberate manual phases with an exit plan.
- **Applies when:** Pre-PMF through early growth.
- **Limitations:** Unscalable work must be paired with learning goals, or it becomes a services business by accident.
- **Skills:** EV, PG

### Startup = Growth (Sep 2012)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html
- **Thesis:** What makes a startup a startup is that it's designed to grow fast. Everything else follows from growth.
- **Principles:** Pick one key metric (ideally revenue) and measure weekly growth. Graham cites 5–7% a week as good and 10% as exceptional in the YC context. Growth targets act as a compass for decisions.
- **Implications:** Every evaluation names the metric that matters and its rate, with its base.
- **Applies when:** Venture-backed models. Less relevant to deliberately small businesses.
- **Limitations:** Weekly benchmarks don't transfer to enterprise sales cycles or regulated launches.
- **Skills:** EV, FR, PD

### How to Start a Startup (Mar 2005)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/start.html
- **Thesis:** You need three things: good people, something customers actually want, and as little money spent as possible.
- **Principles:** Founders matter most. Make something people want. Stay cheap, because a low burn buys time to find what works.
- **Applies when:** Framing team, product and capital decisions together.
- **Limitations:** Written before cloud infrastructure and modern seed markets. The specifics are dated; the principles are not.
- **Skills:** EV, FR

### The Hardest Lessons for Startups to Learn (Apr 2006)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html
- **Thesis:** Several counter-intuitive lessons recur: release early, keep pumping out features, make users happy, fear the right things, commitment is a self-fulfilling prophecy, there is always room, don't get your hopes up, and speed matters.
- **Implications:** Use as a checklist against founders' stated plans, e.g. a delayed launch or a fear of competitors.
- **Skills:** EV

### Default Alive or Default Dead? (Oct 2015)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html
- **Thesis:** Ask whether, on current expense levels and revenue growth, the company reaches profitability before the money runs out. Many founders don't know the answer.
- **Principles:** If default-dead, change course early. Don't assume the next round will happen. Over-hiring is the common cause of being default dead.
- **Implications:** The fundraising strategist and the financial model always compute runway and the default-alive test under stated assumptions.
- **Limitations:** Deep-tech and long-R&D companies can be default-dead by design. Then the question becomes milestone financing risk.
- **Skills:** FR, PG

### The Fatal Pinch (Jul 2014)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/pinch.html
- **Thesis:** Being default-dead while expecting to raise more money is a dangerous combination, especially when the startup doesn't look impressive enough to raise.
- **Principles:** Assume you won't be able to raise. Cut expenses and focus on growth to become default alive.
- **Skills:** FR

### Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule (Jul 2009)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html
- **Thesis:** Makers need long uninterrupted blocks, and a single meeting can wreck a maker's half-day. Managers work in hour-long slots.
- **Implications:** Fundraising plans should batch investor meetings so build time stays protected.
- **Skills:** FR

### Founder Mode (Sep 2024)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/foundermode.html
- **Thesis:** Conventional "hire good people and give them room" management advice can fail founders as companies scale. Founders may need to stay more directly involved, in a mode that is still poorly understood.
- **Limitations:** Graham describes this as an emerging and not yet well-defined idea. Don't treat it as a framework.
- **Skills:** EV (team assessment, used cautiously)

### Schlep Blindness (Jan 2012)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/schlep.html
- **Thesis:** People unconsciously avoid ideas that involve tedious, unpleasant work. That blindness leaves valuable problems open, with Stripe and payments as the example.
- **Implications:** Hard, regulated, operational problems such as payments, compliance and logistics can be *more* attractive because others avoid them.
- **Skills:** EV, RS

### Organic Startup Ideas (Apr 2010)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/organic.html
- **Thesis:** The best ideas tend to come from asking "what do I wish someone would make for me?" rather than "what could I build a startup around?"
- **Skills:** EV

### Ramen Profitable (Jul 2009)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html
- **Thesis:** Making just enough to cover the founders' minimal living costs changes the company's position. It no longer depends on investors, and its attitude to fundraising changes.
- **Implications:** For capital-scarce ecosystems, ramen profitability is often the most realistic first milestone.
- **Skills:** FR, PG

### The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups (Oct 2006)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html
- **Thesis:** A catalogue of common failure causes, including single founder, bad location, marginal niche, derivative idea, obstinacy, hiring bad programmers, choosing the wrong platform, slowness in launching, launching too early, having no specific user in mind, raising too little money, spending too much, raising too much, poor investor management, sacrificing users to (supposed) profit, not wanting to get your hands dirty, fights between founders, and a half-hearted effort.
- **Implications:** Used as a risk checklist in the evaluator's risk register.
- **Skills:** EV

### How to Raise Money (Sep 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html
- **Thesis:** A practical guide to fundraising. Raising is distracting and unpredictable, investors follow other investors, and founders should get the first commitment and build momentum while keeping the company growing.
- **Skills:** FR

### How to Convince Investors (Aug 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/convince.html
- **Thesis:** Investors are looking for formidable founders working on a promising market. Explain clearly and honestly why the startup could become big. Don't perform confidence.
- **Skills:** FR, PD

### Investor Herd Dynamics (Sep 2013)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/herd.html
- **Thesis:** Investors' decisions depend heavily on other investors. Momentum and the first committed investor drive rounds.
- **Skills:** FR

### Relentlessly Resourceful (Mar 2009)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/relres.html
- **Thesis:** The quality Graham most associates with successful founders is being relentlessly resourceful: neither passive nor merely determined, but energetically finding ways around obstacles.
- **Skills:** EV (founder assessment)

### Before the Startup (Oct 2014)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/before.html
- **Thesis:** Startups are counter-intuitive. You can't game investors or users, because the only reliable path is making something people want. Know your users deeply.
- **Skills:** EV

### Mean People Fail (Nov 2014)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/mean.html
- **Thesis:** In startups, as in the wider modern economy, being mean is a handicap. Good founders tend to be benevolent, and that helps with recruiting and investors.
- **Skills:** EV

### How Not to Die (Aug 2007)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/die.html
- **Thesis:** Startups rarely die from competitors. The common cause is founders giving up. Survival lets luck and learning compound.
- **Skills:** EV, FR

### How to Present to Investors (Aug 2006)
- **URL:** https://www.paulgraham.com/investors.html
- **Thesis:** Practical presentation advice: explain what you're doing plainly, focus on the core idea, show rather than tell, and expect investors to be judging the founders.
- **Skills:** PD
