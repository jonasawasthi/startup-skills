# Problem-solution fit

## Attributed (Osterwalder, 2012)

The Value Proposition Canvas zooms in on two blocks of the Business Model Canvas: the customer segment and the value proposition.

- The **customer profile** lists the customer's *jobs* (functional, social and emotional), *pains* (ranked by intensity and frequency) and *gains*.
- The **value map** lists *products and services*, *pain relievers* and *gain creators*.
- **Fit** is achieved when the value map addresses the jobs, pains and gains that matter most.

Osterwalder presents the canvas as a thinking tool, to be combined with testing: it makes the hypotheses explicit, prepares customer interviews, and guides changes of direction.

## Assessment rules (original synthesis)

The engine assesses nine dimensions:

1. existence;
2. frequency;
3. severity;
4. urgency;
5. dissatisfaction with existing solutions;
6. demand for alternatives;
7. solution relevance;
8. behaviour change;
9. purchase commitment.

Each dimension is rated from the hypotheses mapped to it (`dimension:` in the register) and their evidence: **supported**, **contradicted**, **untested** or **mixed**.

| Verdict | Requires |
|---|---|
| Insufficient evidence | Problem existence not supported by interview or dataset evidence |
| Problem supported; solution untested | Existence plus severity or frequency supported; solution relevance untested |
| Early problem-solution signals | Problem dimensions supported, and solution relevance supported by observed use (prototype, concierge) |
| Problem-solution fit supported (provisional) | All of the above, plus behaviour change or purchase commitment supported by `committed` or `paid` evidence, with no contradicted problem dimension |

**Never declared from interest alone.** Interviews in which people *liked the idea* support nothing above "problem supported". Fit "on paper", where the canvas looks complete, is a set of hypotheses.
