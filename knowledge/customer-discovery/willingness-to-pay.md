# Willingness-to-pay research

See also: [GTM pricing](../gtm/pricing-and-monetization.md) and [YC pricing](../yc/pricing.md).

## Attributed

- ***Monetizing Innovation*** (Ramanujam and Tacke, Wiley, 2016). The book's subtitle states its thesis: design the product around the price, rather than pricing it after it is built. Simon-Kucher's page frames the problem: many innovations miss their financial targets, and the authors argue this should not be accepted as a normal cost of doing business. The book's detailed methods were not re-read for this file.
- **YC (Blomfield, 2024)** covers how to arrive at a B2B price and justify it to customers.

## Evidence ladder (original synthesis)

The engine checks the `signal:` field on ledger entries. A pricing or willingness-to-pay hypothesis can be marked *supported* only with `committed` or `paid` evidence.

| Signal | Example | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| `stated` | "I'd pay about 500"; survey answers | Weak: hypothetical |
| `observed` | Current spend on alternatives; a price they paid last year | Medium: real money, a different product |
| `committed` | A deposit, pre-order, signed LOI with a price, or a scheduled paid pilot | Strong |
| `paid` | A completed payment for this offer | Strongest |

**Questions that produce `observed` evidence:**

- "What do you pay today for [alternative]? Who approved it?"
- "What did you pay the last time you solved this?"
- "What happened the last time a price for this went up?"

**Methods that produce `committed` evidence:** pre-orders, deposits, paid pilots, and a price shown on a checkout before the product ships (with an honest disclosure and a refund).

**Never treat interest as purchase intent.** Waitlist sign-ups and survey answers cannot support a price.
