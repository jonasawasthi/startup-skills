# Emerging-market and cross-border checklist

Use this checklist when a startup operates in, or expands to, markets such as Nepal, India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, Africa or LATAM. **This file lists the questions to research. It does not supply the answers.** Regulations change, so cite current primary sources.

## Payments and money movement
- Which domestic payment rails and wallets do customers actually use? Find each provider's merchant terms and fees from primary sources.
- Can the startup legally hold or route customer funds, and which licence (payment service provider, e-money, aggregator) does that require? Who is the licensed partner?
- Payout mechanics to creators or sellers: settlement time, fees, KYC requirements.
- Cross-border flows: can foreign customers pay (cards, PayPal availability), and can revenue be repatriated? What are the FX controls?

## Corporate and investment
- What are the foreign direct investment rules, minimum investment thresholds and approval processes? Which sectors are restricted?
- Are SAFEs or convertible instruments recognised under local law? Is a holding structure common (e.g. a Delaware, Singapore or other parent) and what does it cost?
- What approval is needed to repatriate investment returns?

## Data, content and tax
- Data protection law and data-localisation requirements.
- Platform liability for user content (creator platforms especially).
- VAT/GST on digital services, withholding on payouts to creators, and tax on cross-border digital services.

## Market structure
- Smartphone and internet penetration, data costs, and connectivity in target regions (cite official telecom statistics).
- Language and script support (e.g. Devanagari rendering), and literacy considerations.
- Diaspora markets: remittance corridors and diaspora demand for home-country creators and products.

## Expansion logic
- Sequence markets by the shared factors of payments, language, diaspora and regulation, not by size alone.
- For each new market, re-run TAM/SAM/SOM with local evidence. Never scale a home-market figure by population.
