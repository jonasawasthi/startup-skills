# Marketplace and multi-sided analysis

## Questions

1. **Which side is harder to acquire?** That side constrains growth. Subsidise or seed it.
2. **Atomic network:** what is the smallest network that delivers value (one creator and their members, one city, one cooperative)? Can it be built by hand?
3. **Liquidity:** match rate, time to match and market depth in the atomic network (a16z metrics 1–3).
4. **Take rate defensibility:** what stops disintermediation after the first match? (payments, trust, tooling, protection, discovery)
5. **Multi-tenanting:** do both sides use competing platforms at once? If yes, defensibility is low.
6. **Concentration:** do a few suppliers drive most volume? If yes, they hold the bargaining power.
7. **Retention by cohort and by location:** improving retention as networks mature is evidence of network effects.

## Creator-economy specifics

- **Creator economics first.** Payout share, payout speed and ease of moving an existing audience decide adoption.
- **Supply brings demand.** Each creator imports an audience, so the hard side is often *creator activation*, not member acquisition.
- **Portability tension.** Creators want to own their audience, and platforms want lock-in. Value-added tools (payments, analytics, community, merchandise, bookings) earn retention better than lock-in does.
- **Payments and payouts** are often the real moat in markets where local payment rails are fragmented. They are also the main regulatory exposure.
