# Competitive analysis

## Map the alternatives (all four layers)

1. **Direct competitors**: same job, same customer, similar product.
2. **Indirect competitors**: same job, different approach (agencies, horizontal tools).
3. **Substitutes**: a different way to satisfy the underlying need.
4. **The status quo**: spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, paper, cash, doing nothing. This is usually the strongest competitor early on.

## For each competitor, record (with evidence and the date checked)

Who they serve · pricing · geography · distribution · strengths · weaknesses · funding (from a verifiable source only) · recent moves · **how they would respond** if you succeed.

## Positioning

- Choose 2–4 **criteria that customers use to decide**. Use your interview evidence, not the criteria you happen to win on.
- The comparison matrix uses yes/partial/no/planned. **Planned** capabilities are shown as planned for both you *and* competitors.
- The 2×2 positioning map is a qualitative judgement and is labelled as such.

## Honesty rules

- Never claim "no competitors". The status quo always competes.
- Never understate competitors with stale information. Re-check pricing and features before each use. The ledger flags entries older than the staleness window.
- Competitor funding, revenue or user numbers need a public source. Otherwise omit them.
