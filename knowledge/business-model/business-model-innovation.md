# Business model innovation

## Attributed

- **Johnson, Christensen and Kagermann (HBR, 2008)**, "Reinventing Your Business Model": the visible summary argues that recognising when a business needs a fundamental change is key, and that established companies find business model innovation hard partly because they do not understand their current model. It opens with Apple's iPod and iTunes combination. The full article is paywalled; its framework is summarised in [the earlier file](../other/business-model-innovation.md) and was not re-read here.
- The **Christensen Institute** maintains its theory of disruptive innovation; the page was checked by URL and title only.

## Original synthesis: when to change the model, and how to compare options

- **Change when evidence says the current one fails**, not when a new pattern looks exciting: a fee customers refuse, a channel that never pays back, a cost that grows faster than revenue, or a dependency that cannot be verified.
- **Vary one thing at a time** (who pays, what they pay for, when, or the operating model) so each alternative isolates a different bet.
- **Compare on defined qualitative scales** with the reasoning and evidence for each rating. Numerical scoring of alternatives invents precision the evidence does not have.
- **Recommend a model to test, not a model that works.** The recommendation lists the assumptions to test before committing money, and the evidence that would make the founder switch to a viable alternative.
