# Value Proposition Canvas

See also: [jobs to be done](../customer-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md), [problem-solution fit](../customer-discovery/problem-solution-fit.md), [business model canvas](business-model-canvas.md).

## Attributed

- **Strategyzer** splits the canvas into a **customer profile** for one segment (the jobs customers are trying to get done, the pains they meet, the gains they want) and a **value map** (products and services, pain relievers, gain creators). The page treats fit as an assumption until customers confirm it: the matches drawn between profile and map are checked against real evidence and the design adjusted. *Value Proposition Design* (2014) develops the method; it was not re-read for this file.
- The **jobs-to-be-done** lens (Christensen Institute) frames what customers "hire" a product to do; see the customer discovery file for detail.

## Original synthesis

- **One canvas per segment.** In a marketplace the supply and demand sides have different jobs and pains; merging them hides the harder side.
- **Profile items come from customers.** Without interviews or observed behaviour, jobs, pains and gains are hypotheses and must be labelled that way; linking them to the discovery hypothesis register keeps one list of what to test.
- **Make the mapping explicit.** Give pains and gains ids and make each pain reliever and gain creator say which it addresses. Unaddressed top pains are design gaps; relievers that address no pain are features without a reason.
- **Fit is a ladder, not a checkbox:** conceptual only → customer evidence exists → problem side evidenced → early evidence of fit (value-map items backed by usage, commitment or payment). Even the last step is early.
