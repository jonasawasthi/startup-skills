# Business Model Canvas

See also: [value proposition canvas](value-proposition-canvas.md), [validation and evidence](validation-and-evidence.md), [HBR business model innovation](../other/business-model-innovation.md).

## Attributed

- **Strategyzer** describes the canvas as a one-page tool for describing how a business creates, delivers and captures value, originating in *Business Model Generation* (Osterwalder and Pigneur, Wiley, 2010). The page frames the right side as the market and the left side as what it takes to serve that market, with value propositions between them, and advises treating a canvas as "a claim, not a plan": date and version it, and redraw it as evidence comes in. It also notes that cost structure and revenue streams only hold if the other blocks are right. The book itself was not re-read for this file.
- **Steve Blank** defines a startup as an organisation formed to *search* for a repeatable and scalable business model, and treats each part of the model as a hypothesis to be tested with customers: "Most of the time the darn customers don't behave as you predicted."

## The nine blocks (standard names)

Customer Segments · Value Propositions · Channels · Customer Relationships · Revenue Streams · Key Resources · Key Activities · Key Partnerships · Cost Structure.

## Original synthesis: a canvas with provenance

A canvas is easy to fill and hard to falsify. This repository's engine therefore attaches a **basis** to every item (evidence, founder-provided, hypothesis, recommendation, unknown) and treats the customer-facing blocks differently from the internal ones:

| Blocks | What counts as evidence |
|---|---|
| Segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue | Customer evidence: interviews, measured behaviour, commitments, payments |
| Resources, activities, partnerships, costs | Documents: code, contracts, invoices, quotes, official pages |

Four states are kept apart: *conceptually coherent* (complete and consistent), *supported by customer evidence*, *economically viable* (paid transactions with positive measured contribution) and *repeatable*. Filling all nine blocks reaches only the first.
