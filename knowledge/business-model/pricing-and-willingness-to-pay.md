# Pricing and willingness to pay in business model design

See also: [willingness-to-pay research](../customer-discovery/willingness-to-pay.md) (the evidence ladder), [GTM pricing](../gtm/pricing-and-monetization.md), [YC pricing](../yc/pricing.md).

## Attributed

- **Gurley (2013):** a platform's take rate is a strategic choice; a rake that is too high invites suppliers to leave and competitors to undercut.
- ***Monetizing Innovation*** (Ramanujam and Tacke): design the product around the price rather than pricing after building. Summarised in the customer discovery knowledge base.
- **YC (Blomfield, 2024):** how to arrive at and justify a B2B price.

## Original synthesis: pricing in the model

- **Every price starts as a hypothesis.** It becomes evidence only when customers commit or pay at that price (`signal: committed` or `paid`). Stated interest, survey answers and waitlists are `stated`.
- **In a marketplace there are at least two prices:** what the buyer pays, and what the supplier gives up (the take rate). Both need testing; supplier acceptance of the fee is a separate assumption from buyer willingness to pay.
- **Check the floor arithmetically.** The lowest viable price is the one at which a transaction still contributes after payment fees, delivery and support. If the planned floor sits close to it, small cost surprises (VAT, fee rates, support) erase the margin.
- **Packaging changes the model.** Bundles, tiers and subscriptions shift revenue timing and retention needs; record each as a stream with its own stage.
