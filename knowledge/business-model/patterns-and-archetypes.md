# Business model patterns and archetypes

See also: [revenue architecture](revenue-architecture.md), [marketplace business models](marketplace-business-models.md), [GTM by business model](../gtm/gtm-by-business-model.md).

## Attributed

- **a16z, "16 Startup Metrics" (2015)** stresses that investors value revenue that comes from the product rather than services, because services revenue is non-recurring, lower-margin and scales poorly; and that recurring revenue should exclude one-time and professional-service fees.
- **Gurley (2012)** lists factors that make some marketplaces more attractive than others; the post was not re-read for this file.
- **Van Alstyne, Parker and Choudary (HBR, 2016)** contrast pipeline businesses with platforms; the article is paywalled and was not read.

## Taxonomy used by the engine (original)

| Dimension | Types |
|---|---|
| Revenue model | SaaS, subscription, freemium, usage-based, transaction-based, commission-based, digital products, courses and education, e-commerce, service-based, consulting, advertising-supported, licensing |
| Structure | marketplace, direct sales, hybrid |
| Orientation | B2B, B2C, B2B2C |

A **hybrid** combines revenue models from different families (recurring, usage, transaction, one-time, services, advertising, licensing). SaaS plus subscription is one family, not a hybrid.

## Structural requirements each type implies (original)

| Type | Must also have |
|---|---|
| Marketplace | Separate sides, a liquidity assumption, a payment collection and payout dependency |
| Commission / transaction | A take rate or fee, a payment dependency, a payment-processing cost |
| SaaS / subscription | A recurring revenue stream and a retention assumption |
| Freemium | A paid upgrade and a free-to-paid conversion assumption |
| Advertising | An advertising stream and an audience-volume assumption (rarely viable early) |
| Services / consulting | A service-fee stream and a delivery-capacity assumption |
| E-commerce | One-time sales and a direct delivery cost |
| Direct sales | An acquisition cost |

Each type's fit should be argued in words: who pays, why they pay that way, and what has to be true.
