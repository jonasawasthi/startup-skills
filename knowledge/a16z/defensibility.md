# Defensibility

## Data moats are weaker than they look (Casado and Lauten, 2019)

- True data *network* effects are rare. Most "data flywheels" are **scale effects**.
- The marginal value of data tends to **diminish**, while the cost of acquiring and cleaning it rises.
- Data goes stale, and competitors can collect similar data.
- Real data advantages exist: proprietary sources, access gated by compliance or security, and trust as a responsible custodian.
- More reliable moats include differentiated technology, deep vertical domain knowledge, dominance of go-to-market, and the team.

## AI businesses differ from classic software (Casado and Bornstein, 2020; URL verified)

The article argues that many AI companies have lower gross margins (compute and human-in-the-loop costs), scaling challenges from edge cases, and weaker defensibility than traditional SaaS. Evaluate AI startups on gross margin and on how much of the value is proprietary rather than coming from the model.

## Defensibility checklist (original synthesis)

| Source of advantage | Evidence required | Strength early |
|---|---|---|
| Network effects (type named per NFX) | Retention or engagement improving with network size, by cohort or location | Strong once proven |
| Switching costs | Workflows or data embedded; migration effort | Medium |
| Proprietary data or access | Contracts, licences, regulatory access | Medium–strong |
| Regulatory licence | Licence held (not applied for) | Strong where required |
| Brand / community | Organic acquisition share, NPS | Weak early |
| Technology | Something hard to replicate, beyond "we use AI" | Varies |
| Distribution | A channel others can't access | Often the real early moat |
| Speed / execution | Shipping cadence | Temporary |

**Rule:** "Network effects" may only be claimed with a named type and supporting metrics. Otherwise the deck says "potential network effects (unproven)".
