# MVP

## Attributed principles

- Launch quickly. A product with a real "quantum of utility" beats a polished product that ships late (YC essential advice).
- Look for the 90/10 solution: most of the value for a fraction of the build (Buchheit, via YC).
- Seibel's MVP talk is aimed at pre-launch founders. Its library page lists the situations it addresses: founders who haven't built an MVP yet, are choosing features, have built something nobody uses yet, or are taking longer than expected to launch.
- In a good market, the MVP only needs to get initial customers in the door and generating feedback (Seibel, *The real product-market fit*).

## MVP scoping checklist (original synthesis)

1. **One user, one job.** Name the single user role and the single job-to-be-done.
2. **One success metric** that shows the job got done, e.g. "deliveries recorded per agent per week".
3. **Timebox** the build in weeks, not months. If it can't fit, cut scope until it does.
4. **Manual behind the curtain.** Which parts can a human do for the first 10 customers (concierge MVP)?
5. **Explicit non-goals.** List the features you will *not* build yet.
6. **Kill and continue criteria** decided *before* launch (see [validation-experiments](../frameworks/validation-experiments.md)).

## Limitations

Regulated domains (payments, health, finance) have a minimum *compliant* product that may be larger than a pure MVP. In those domains, separate *learning* MVPs (prototypes, concierge pilots) from *launchable* products.
