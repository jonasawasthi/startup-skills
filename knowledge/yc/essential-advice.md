# YC's essential startup advice: summary and application

## Core principles (paraphrased)

| Principle | Practical implication for our skills |
|---|---|
| **Launch now.** Ship a mediocre product early, provided it carries a real "quantum of utility". | Evaluator: an idea with no launch path in weeks is a risk to flag. Deck: show what has shipped, not what is planned. |
| **Do things that don't scale.** Get the first customers by hand. | Validation plans should include manual and concierge steps. |
| **Find the 90/10 solution** (credited to Paul Buchheit): 90% of the value for 10% of the effort. | MVP recommendations should name what to cut. |
| **Write code and talk to users.** Almost everything else is a distraction early on. | The readiness assessment checks the founders' time allocation. |
| **10–100 customers who love you beat 1,000 who kind of like you.** Sometimes you must fire customers. | The ICP must be narrow. Early adopters are the people with a burning problem. |
| **Growth follows product-market fit; it doesn't precede it.** Growing an unprofitable product burns cash (unit economics matter). | Don't recommend paid acquisition before retention is evident. |
| **Pick one or two key metrics** and choose work by its effect on them. | Every evaluation should name the metric that matters. |
| **All startups are badly broken at some point.** | Weaknesses are normal. Judge whether the founders can fix them, not whether they exist. |
| **Ignore competitors early.** Startups "die of suicide, not murder." | Competitive analysis informs positioning. It is rarely the main risk in year one. |
| **Raise quickly, then get back to work. Valuation ≠ success. It's not your money.** | Fundraising strategy aims to minimise time spent raising and dilution theatre. |
| **Founder relationships matter. Be nice. Look after your health.** | Founder-market fit includes team durability. |

## Limitations

- The advice is calibrated to software startups that can launch in weeks. Regulated, hardware, deep-tech and marketplace businesses with heavy supply-side requirements need adapted "launch" definitions (e.g. a pilot or a concierge test).
- "Ignore competitors" applies to *attention*, not to analysis. Investors still expect a clear competitive answer.
