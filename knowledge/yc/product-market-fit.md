# Product-market fit (PMF)

## Definitions

- **Andreessen (2007):** PMF means being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market. When you have it, customers buy as fast as you can deliver and usage outruns capacity. Andreessen argues that the market matters most of the three ingredients (team, product, market), and that "the market pulls product out of the startup".
- **Seibel (YC):** you have PMF when you are *overwhelmed* with usage, to the point where keeping the product running crowds out major changes. Headcount and funding are not signs of PMF. Until you have it, stay lean, keep burn low and stay small. Find PMF by focusing on the market (the problem) first. Founders hold "too tightly onto solutions and too loosely onto problems".
- **Sequoia's Arc framework** describes PMF in three archetypes: *hair on fire* (urgent, known problem), *hard fact* (accepted pain people don't expect to be fixed) and *future vision* (new behaviour). Each archetype implies a different go-to-market. See [sequoia/pmf-arc.md](../sequoia/pmf-arc.md).

## Evidence ladder used by Startup Skills (original synthesis)

| Level | Evidence | How to present |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Opinions, waitlist sign-ups | "Interest", never "traction" |
| 1 | Repeated usage by a few users (cohort retention visible) | "Early usage" with the cohort chart |
| 2 | Users pay, or hand over real workflows and data | "Early revenue / design partners" |
| 3 | Retention curves flatten above zero; organic referrals | "Signs of PMF", with the evidence |
| 4 | Demand exceeds capacity; growth without proportional spend | "PMF", stated with metrics |

The pitch deck maker refuses to use the phrase "product-market fit" unless the ledger contains level 3+ evidence.

## Limitations

PMF is a continuum and is segment-specific. A product can have PMF in one segment or geography and none in another. Claims should always name the segment.
