# Growth

## Attributed ideas

- **Startup = Growth (Graham, 2012):** a startup is a company designed to grow fast, and that is what distinguishes it from a small business. Graham suggests measuring weekly growth in a single key metric (ideally revenue). He cites 5–7% a week as a good rate during YC and 10% as exceptional, at least early on. Choosing the metric and the target focuses the whole company.
- **Do Things That Don't Scale (Graham, 2013):** startups take off because founders *make* them take off. That means recruiting users manually, delivering an "insanely great" experience to early users, and focusing on a narrow initial market.
- **Growth follows product, not the reverse (YC essential advice).** Growing before PMF leads to poor retention. Growing an unprofitable product drains cash.
- **Fundraising signal (Ralston):** 10% per week for several weeks is impressive to seed investors.

## How Startup Skills applies this

- The evaluator and deck maker ask for **the one metric that matters** and its weekly or monthly growth, with evidence.
- Growth rates are always shown with the base number ("+40% MoM from 12 to 17 users") so small bases are not dressed up.
- If no growth data exists, the deck uses *progress* (shipped milestones, pilots, LOIs) and says so plainly.

## Limitations

Weekly-growth benchmarks come from consumer and software startups in an accelerator setting. Enterprise, hardware, regulated and emerging-market businesses grow in steps: pilot → contract → rollout. Evaluate those on milestone velocity instead.
