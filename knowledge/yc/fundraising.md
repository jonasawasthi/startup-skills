# Seed fundraising

## When to raise (Ralston)

Investors write cheques when three things hold: the idea is compelling, the team can realise it, and the opportunity is real and large. For most founders, persuading them takes a product and traction. Ralston says to raise when you understand the market and the customer, have delivered a product that matches their needs, and are seeing *interestingly rapid* adoption. He gives about 10% a week for several weeks as an example of impressive growth.

## How much (Ralston)

Ideally raise enough to reach profitability. In practice, raise enough to reach the milestones that make the next round (or default-alive status) achievable. Tie the amount to a concrete plan, typically 12–24 months of runway. See also Graham's *Default Alive or Default Dead*.

## Instruments

- **SAFE** (Simple Agreement for Future Equity), created by YC. Its standard documents are published at https://www.ycombinator.com/documents. Post-money SAFEs make dilution easy to calculate.
- **Convertible notes** are debt that converts to equity. They carry interest and a maturity date.
- **Priced equity rounds** cost more in legal work and are more common from Series A.

Local law determines which instruments can be used. In many non-US jurisdictions, SAFEs are not recognised or face foreign-investment rules. The fundraising strategist must flag this; see [emerging-markets](../frameworks/emerging-markets.md).

## Rules to follow (Ralston, paraphrased)

- Finish fundraising quickly and get back to building, but don't stop too soon if it's hard.
- Be "greedy": search breadth-first, weighted by likelihood to close.
- When someone says yes, close immediately (sign the documents, get the money in).
- Never mislead anyone. Reputation compounds.
- Learn to hear "no" in its many forms. Part on good terms.
- Stay organised, and split the work between co-founders.

## Don'ts with investors (Ralston, paraphrased)

Don't be dishonest, arrogant or indecisive. Don't oversell. Don't present detailed financials at seed or market sizes without justification. Don't claim knowledge you lack, ask for an NDA, negotiate in real time, or play investors off each other unless you are very experienced.

## Graham's fundraising essays (themes)

- *How to Raise Money* (2013): fundraising is a distraction and is unpredictable. Investors are influenced by other investors. Get the first commitment, then use momentum. Avoid letting fundraising stall product work.
- *How to Convince Investors* (2013): investors are persuaded by **formidable founders** with a **promising market**. The best way to seem formidable is to *be* formidable and to explain plainly why the idea could grow big.
- *Investor Herd Dynamics* (2013): investors follow one another, so the first credible commitment matters most.

## How Startup Skills applies this

- *fundraising-strategist* produces a readiness assessment against these criteria, a milestone-based raise amount (never invented; the founder decides), and an instrument note that names the jurisdiction.
- The *pitch-deck-maker* never uses "ridiculous market size numbers without clear justification". Bottom-up sizing is required.
