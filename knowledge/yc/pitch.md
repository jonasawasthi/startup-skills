# Pitching (YC)

## Seibel's seven questions

A clear pitch answers these, in this order:

1. **What do you do?** Lead with the company name and a plain description. Skip the problem setup. If it's hard to explain, walk through the user's path.
2. **How big is the market?**
3. **What's your progress?**
4. **What's your unique insight?**
5. **What's your business model?**
6. **Who's on your team?**
7. **What do you want?** (the ask)

## Harris's seed deck template (2018)

Harris built the template to help founders lay out their stories in slides, and notes the seed deck differs little from a Series A deck. His emphasis is **clarity and concision**: "Focus on narrative." Most seed companies have little meaningful detail, and pretending otherwise muddles the story.

The sequence is: **Title** (name + one line) → **Problem** (real-world particulars) → **Solution** (concrete benefits, few words) → **Traction** (clear, meaningful numbers, with context) → **More metrics** → **Unique insight** → **Business model** → **Market** → **Team** (founders; advisors don't matter at seed) → **Ask** (how much and what it gets you, ideally reaching Series A readiness within about a year).

Other guidance from Harris: each section may run to a set of slides, but keep each set to three or fewer. The template is deliberately simple in design.

## How Startup Skills applies this

- The pitch deck maker's *seed* narrative follows this order by default, with the cover subtitle answering Seibel's question 1.
- The "simple design" rule informs the design system: one idea per slide, an action title, minimal text, real numbers only.
- Speaker notes are written as answers to the seven questions so founders can rehearse.

## Limitations

The template is written for Demo Day follow-ups and US seed investors. Corporate, grant and government audiences need different emphasis, which the proposal generator handles.
