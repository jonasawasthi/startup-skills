# Pricing

## Attributed

Hale's *Startup Pricing 101* covers the fundamentals of pricing and monetisation and how pricing shapes customer acquisition strategy. The library page recommends it for founders who aren't sure how to price, whose unit economics aren't working, or who worry their market is too small. A common theme in summaries of the talk is that early startups usually **charge too little**. Price determines how much you can spend to acquire a customer.

## Pricing analysis method (original synthesis)

1. **Value anchor:** quantify the customer's cost of the problem (time × wage, losses, fees). Price is a fraction of the value created.
2. **Alternative anchor:** what does the customer pay today for the workaround?
3. **Willingness-to-pay evidence:** pre-sales, paid pilots, or the price at which deals were lost. Interview statements alone are weak.
4. **Acquisition constraint:** price × gross margin × expected lifetime must comfortably exceed CAC (see [unit-economics](../frameworks/unit-economics.md)). Low prices force low-touch channels.
5. **Local purchasing power:** in emerging markets, benchmark against local SaaS and service prices, not US prices. Use PPP adjustments only as a sanity check.
6. **Packaging:** use 2–3 tiers that reflect segments, and keep one dominant plan.

## Limitations

Marketplaces price by take rate. The relevant constraints are the supply side's margin and how easily the market can be disintermediated. See [marketplace-analysis](../frameworks/marketplace-analysis.md).
