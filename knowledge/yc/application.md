# YC-style application answers

The public apply page sends applicants to apply.ycombinator.com and says that promising applicants are interviewed by video. The form changes between batches. **Always copy the questions from the live form.** Don't rely on this list.

## Question themes that commonly appear

| Theme | Purpose (our analysis) | What a strong answer does |
|---|---|---|
| Describe the company in very few words | Tests clarity | Plain words: what it is, for whom. No adjectives. |
| What are you making? | Tests concreteness | Walks through what a user actually does with it |
| Why this idea, and what domain expertise do you have? | Founder–market fit, insight | Shows a personal encounter with the problem or a non-obvious insight |
| Who are the competitors, and what do you understand that they don't? | Market awareness, insight | Names real alternatives, including the status quo, and one sharp insight |
| How will you make money, and how much could you make? | Business model, ambition | A simple model with a bottom-up estimate and its assumptions shown |
| How far along are you? Users, revenue, growth | Progress, honesty | Exact numbers, dates and growth rates. "Pre-launch" is fine when it's true |
| Something impressive each founder has built or achieved | Formidability | Specific, verifiable, preferably relevant |
| Equity split and commitment | Team durability | Clear and fair. Founders full-time or with a date |

## Qualities of strong answers (consistent with Seibel's pitching guidance)

1. **Plain language.** Jargon hides weak understanding.
2. **Specific numbers with dates.** Never round up, and never blend projections into progress.
3. **Insight over adjectives.** "We noticed X because Y" beats "revolutionary platform".
4. **Honest about stage.** Reviewers read thousands of applications, and inflation is easy to spot.
5. **Short.** Answer the question asked, then stop.
