# Talking to users

## Principles (attributed)

- Founders must talk to users themselves, continuously. "Write code and talk to users" is YC's core loop (YC essential advice).
- Migicovsky's Startup School lecture, as widely summarised, advises: don't pitch your idea in an interview; ask about specific past behaviour, not hypotheticals; listen far more than you talk.
- Early recruitment is manual and unscalable (Graham, *Do Things That Don't Scale*).

## Interview protocol used by Startup Skills (original synthesis)

**Goal:** learn whether a problem is frequent, painful and currently paid for. The goal is *not* to collect compliments.

1. **Anchor in the last occurrence:** "Tell me about the last time you had to [do X]."
2. **Map the workflow:** "What did you do next? Which tools did you use? Who else was involved?"
3. **Measure the cost:** "How long did it take? What did it cost? What happens when it goes wrong?"
4. **Find existing spending:** "What have you tried or paid for to fix this?" Money and time already spent are the strongest signals.
5. **Gauge priority:** "Where does this rank among the problems you're dealing with this month?"
6. **Ask for a commitment** (only at the end): a pilot, a pre-order, an introduction, or data access.

| Signal strength | Example |
|---|---|
| Strong | Already pays for a workaround. Asks when they can use it. Introduces colleagues. Pre-pays. |
| Medium | Describes a recent, specific, costly incident. Agrees to a pilot with their own data. |
| Weak | "That's a great idea." "I would definitely use that." Hypothetical future behaviour. |

**Recording.** Each interview becomes an evidence entry with `source.type: interview`, the date, the interviewee's role (no personal data in public files), and a note on whether the finding is *behavioural* (strong) or *opinion* (weak).

## Limitations

Small samples (fewer than about 10 interviews per segment) show whether a problem exists. They cannot establish its prevalence. Never extrapolate interview counts into market size without a separate data source.
