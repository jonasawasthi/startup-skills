# SaaS economics benchmarks (with provenance)

Benchmarks are **heuristics from specific sources and segments**. Cite the source and year whenever one is used, and never present one as a universal law.

| Metric | Benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CAC payback | ~6–18 months (SMB); ~24–36 months (enterprise) | BVP *10 Laws of Cloud*, 2019 |
| LTV : CAC | ≥ 3× | BVP 2019 |
| Enterprise gross logo churn | < 7% annually | BVP 2019 |
| SMB gross churn | often ~20% annually | BVP 2019 |
| Best-in-class enterprise | < 1% monthly gross churn, > 100% net expansion | BVP 2019 |
| Growth efficiency (< $30M ARR) | net new ARR / net burn > 1.5 | BVP 2019 |
| Low-touch SaaS churn | ~2% to 10%+ monthly | Stripe Atlas (McKenzie) |
| High-touch SaaS churn | ~10% annually | Stripe Atlas (McKenzie) |
| LTV approximation | ARPU / churn (gross-margin adjust for accuracy) | Stripe Atlas |

## Other BVP laws (paraphrased)

Scale wins (leaders capture most of the value). Invest in sales and marketing only behind proven repeatability. Product is the competitive advantage. Find a "secret KPI" (a leading indicator unique to the business). Customer success drives value. Choose between a system of engagement and a system of results. Raise before you need to, with one to three years of runway.

## Stripe Atlas: low-touch vs high-touch

McKenzie distinguishes **low-touch** SaaS (self-serve, low ACV, monthly plans, marketing-led) from **high-touch** SaaS (sales-led, high ACV, annual contracts), plus hybrids. The sales model determines affordable CAC, so pricing and channel must be designed together.
