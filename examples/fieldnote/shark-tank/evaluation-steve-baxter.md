## What attracts

On the documented record (S4, S5), L1 gives more weight to what customers do than to what anyone says about them. Fieldnote's strongest evidence is of exactly that kind: three cooperatives paid a year in advance, confirmed by an invoice export [E-017], and the field app has captured 61,200 records [E-002] (A1). The agent survey [E-016] is a statement of preference, so under this lens it adds little to the payments.

On the documented record (S4, S5, S6), L2 looks for a team whose delivery can be checked. The live product and the paid invoices [E-002, E-017] are checkable (A2); what the ledger does not show is who is on the team and which skills the plan still lacks, which is why D14 is Adequate rather than Strong.

On the documented record (S4, S5), L1 also asks whether the problem needs solving for the people who pay. Three managers describe a recent reconciliation that took days [E-010, E-011, E-013] (A3). The fourth manager, who employs an accountant, does not [E-012], so the evidence supports a segment of cooperatives without accountants rather than the whole registry.

On the documented record (S4, S6), L3 starts from turnover and the cash needed to reach it. A verified price per cooperative [E-017] gives the first input (A4); the second, the cash required, is missing (Q1, Q2).

## Primary concerns

No dimension is rated Weak, so no concern rests on evidence against the business. The concerns are evidence gaps on dimensions that matter most here.

On the documented record (S1, S2, S3, S7), L4 asks whether a technology business can grow without its costs growing in step. Nothing in the ledger measures onboarding or support effort per cooperative (C1), so that cannot yet be judged.

On the documented record (S4, S6), L3 asks for a plan that links projected turnover to the cash needed to reach it. The ledger holds no cash balance, burn or runway (C2).

Two critical dimensions no lens of this profile covers are also open: contribution per cooperative (C3, D07) and the channel and cost of acquisition (C4, D08). Any investor would see these gaps.

## Potential deal-breakers

None identified on current evidence. No lens in this profile carries a documented decline pattern, and no dimension is rated Weak.

## Recommendations

- M1 (D12): build a startup-kit model from bank statements and state when renewals of the first annual cohort will be known. Upgrade condition: bank statements and a model run showing runway to the next milestone. Experiment: none needed; this is a document to assemble.
- M2 (D10): time the next four onboardings and log support hours for eight weeks. Upgrade condition: cost per cooperative falling as cooperatives are added. Experiment: a measurement study whose kill criterion is support time per cooperative that does not fall as cooperatives are added.
- M3 (D03): track renewals of the first annual cohort from billing records. Upgrade condition: renewals or 90-day retention from billing records.
- M4 (D07, base): run the logged costs through startup-kit unit-economics with measured churn.

## What would make the business more compelling under these lenses

A view needs evidence on the four open dimensions first. Under L3, a model that shows the cash in hand lasts until the first annual cohort renews would close D12. Under L4, a falling curve of founder and support hours per cooperative would show technology doing the work as the business grows, the D10 upgrade condition. With those two and the logged cost per cooperative (D07), most of the critical and primary set would be rated, and renewals by the cooperatives that prepaid [E-017] would then test the strongest current evidence under L1.
