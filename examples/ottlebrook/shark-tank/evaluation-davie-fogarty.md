## What attracts

On the documented record (S3, S4), L1 looks for demand that exists before marketing is switched on. Ottlebrook's sell-through of 812 units in 29 weeks [E-007] came with no paid consumer advertising recorded beyond the retailer's co-operative deduction [E-004], and the retailer re-ordered [E-008] (A1). Whether the category is free of strong brands is unknown (Q2).

On the documented record (S2, S3, S4, S5), L3 looks for a repeatable operating system rather than a founder doing everything. The ledger shows two complementary founders (founder-provided) [E-019] and a contracted warehouse [E-006] (A3); it shows no content or customer-service process yet.

## Primary concerns

On the documented record (S3), L2 asks whether gross margin leaves room to buy customers. In the retail channel there is no margin at all [E-009] (C1). The website is where this lens would look for headroom, and there the contribution of about AUD 58 per order is an estimate [E-011] facing an acquisition cost that is only assumed [E-012]: the test L2 favours, small paid spend before scaling, has not been run.

On the documented record (S2, S3, S4, S5), L3 is concerned by growth that rests on one channel without its own customer feedback. Ottlebrook depends on one manufacturer [E-020] and learned of its fault rate from the retailer [E-005] (C3).

The remaining critical weaknesses are base points: the retail terms (C2, re-tagged), dependence on one retailer (C4) and cash for the re-order (C5).

## Recommendations

- M1 (D07): a capped paid-social test to the website, measuring cost per order against contribution per order. Kill criterion: cost per order above the estimated contribution.
- M2 (D11): structured buyer feedback so faults surface early. Upgrade condition for D11: returns below 2% on the next shipment and a second manufacturer sampled.
- M3 (D04): a competitor brand map from shelf visits and listings.

## What would make the business more compelling under these lenses

Under L2, a measured website channel in which cost per order sits well inside contribution per order would give this profile the margin headroom it looks for, independent of the retailer's terms. Under L1, a competitor map showing no dominant heated-airer brand would confirm the kind of early category this lens favours. Under L3, a feedback loop that catches faults before returns would show the operating system growing.

## Outside documented expertise

The profile lists physical retail and wholesale distribution as a known gap. Two candidate L2 points fall inside it and are re-tagged `base`:

- C2 (D07): the effect of the retailer's shelf margin and invoice deductions on profit per unit is a question of wholesale terms, not of paid acquisition.
- A2 (D08): the verified cost of the retail channel is a retailer co-operative marketing deduction, not a paid digital channel.

The L2 point that stays tagged (C1) concerns margin headroom for paid acquisition on the company's own website, which the documented record covers (S3). The profile's other gaps (B2B software, regulated health, deep technology and hardware research) do not apply: Ottlebrook makes a certified consumer appliance and does no research-stage development.
