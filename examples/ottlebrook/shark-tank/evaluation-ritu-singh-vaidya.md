## Stance

**Not persuaded on current evidence.** C1 sits on D12, the dimension this profile puts first, rated Weak: the re-order consumes most of the reported cash before the retailer pays, and each unit loses money, so no path to self-funded operation is shown. C2 adds a primary-dimension weakness under L4, a defect rate with a single supplier, while A3 shows the regulatory groundwork is done.

## What attracts

On the documented record (S9), L2 looks for a concrete execution plan and capable people. The founders' backgrounds fit the product and the channel (founder-provided) [E-019], and they have delivered certification, a listing and a re-order [E-015, E-003, E-008] (A1).

On the documented record (S7, S8, S9), L5 attends to licences and exposure to rule changes. Certification and registration are complete [E-015] (A3); how much of landed cost is import duty is not separated in the ledger [E-002], so exposure to a duty change cannot be measured.

On the documented record (S9), L3 reads growth beyond the home market. The verified larger-order quote [E-017] means more volume could be served at lower cost (A2), but no sale or enquiry outside Australia is recorded.

## Primary concerns

On the documented record (S9, S10), L1 treats profit as the test of whether a business can sustain itself. Ottlebrook does not pass it in its current form: each retail unit loses money [E-009] (C3), the price received is below cost (C5), and fulfilling the re-order would consume most of the reported cash before the retailer pays (C1).

On the documented record (S6, S8, S9, S10), L4 expects after-sales service and a credible supplier to be in place before a manufactured product scales. The ledger shows a 5% fault rate [E-005], one manufacturer with no second source [E-020] and no repair or parts plan (C2). The retail agreement is the kind of written route this lens looks for, but its terms are loss-making (C4).

## Potential deal-breakers

None identified on current evidence. No lens in this profile carries a documented decline pattern.

## Recommendations

- M1 (D12): a cash model from bank statements through the re-order and its payment. Upgrade condition: cash positive through fulfilment on terms that give positive contribution.
- M2 (D11): a supplier agreement with a defect remedy and a second manufacturer sampled. Upgrade condition: returns below 2% on the next shipment.
- M3 (D07): no further orders at the current price unless the larger-order cost is delivered and recalculated. Kill criterion: no combination of price and cost that clears zero contribution.

## What would make the business more compelling under these lenses

Under L1, positive contribution per unit and a cash model that stays positive through the re-order would turn the first test from failed to passed. Under L4, a fault rate below 2% on the next shipment, a parts and repair plan, and a second qualified manufacturer would show the after-sales readiness this lens expects before scale.
