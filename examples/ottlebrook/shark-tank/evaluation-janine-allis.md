Lenses applied (analytical interpretations of the documented record):
- L1 The operator behind the product: the founder's persistence and conduct in diligence weigh as heavily as the product (S4, S5)

## Stance

**Not persuaded on current evidence.** C1 sits on D07, a critical dimension and one this profile puts first, rated Weak on verified evidence: each unit sold through the retailer loses money before any overhead. ## What attracts

On the documented record (S4, S5), L1 looks first for an operator behind the product. Ottlebrook has one that can be checked: the founders certified an appliance, won a national listing and a paid re-order [E-015, E-003, E-008], and report backgrounds in appliance engineering and homewares account management (founder-provided) [E-019] (A1).

On the documented record (S3, S4), L3 values a well-executed answer to a real need over novelty. A heated airer is not new, and that is not held against it: consumers bought 812 units in 29 weeks [E-007], the retailer re-ordered [E-008] (A3), and buyers describe the problem in concrete terms [E-018] (A4).

On the documented record (S3, S6, S7), L2 looks for the lever that makes each outlet profitable. The verified 3,000-unit quote is that lever (A2); it is a quote, not a delivered cost.

## Primary concerns

On the documented record (S3, S6, S7), L2 rests on two things: building a network of hundreds of franchised stores (X1, X2) and the stated view that a business must be financially stable enough to support franchisees before it franchises (P3). Applied to Ottlebrook, the analogue of the store is the unit on a retailer's shelf, and the analogue of rent and royalties is the retailer's margin and deductions; the analogy is this simulation's, not the profile's. On that reading the unit does not stay profitable: contribution is AUD -3.36 per unit [E-009] (C1). The route that produces most sales takes the margin by its terms [E-003, E-004] (C2), and adding doors before the unit is profitable is the expansion-before-stability pattern P3 describes. That the price received sits below the cost of delivering a unit (C3, D06) follows from the shared rating and is recorded as a base point.

Two critical weaknesses that no lens of this profile covers complete the picture: an evidenced defect rate and a single manufacturer (C4, D11), and a re-order that consumes most of the cash reported before the retailer pays (C5, D12).

## Recommendations

- M1 (D07): recalculate contribution at the 3,000-unit quote with real freight, and use it at the next range review. Upgrade condition: a signed wholesale price or a delivered larger order giving positive contribution, recalculated from invoices. Experiment: a costed renegotiation; kill criterion, no price or cost combination that clears zero contribution.
- M2 (D09): add no doors until contribution per unit is positive.
- M3 (D11): root-cause the heating-element fault and sample a second manufacturer. Upgrade condition: returns below 2% on the next shipment and a second source sampled.
- M4 (D12): a cash model from bank statements through the re-order and its payment terms.

## What would make the business more compelling under these lenses

Under L2, one number would change the reading: positive contribution per unit through the retailer, from a revised wholesale price, the lower cost at a larger order, or both, recalculated from invoices rather than quotes. Under L3 the demand evidence already holds, so the case turns on the unit, not the customer. Under L1, the founders' handling of the renegotiation and the defect, with complete figures, is what this lens would read next.
