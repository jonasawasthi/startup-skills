# Source evaluation

## Hierarchy (prefer higher)

1. **Primary data**: databases, analytics, contracts, accounts, codebase.
2. **Official**: statistics offices, regulators, central banks, statutes, filings.
3. **Company primary**: pricing pages, docs, press releases. The company has an interest, so treat performance claims as *reported*.
4. **Reputable research and journalism** with stated methodology and date.
5. **Aggregators, blogs, AI-generated pages, listicles**: only as pointers to primary sources, and recorded as `unverified`.

## Checks for each source

- **Who** published it, and do they have an interest?
- **When** was it published, and what period does the data cover?
- **What exactly** does it measure (definition, geography, units)?
- **How** was it measured (methodology, sample)?
- **Can it be corroborated** independently?

## Status assignment

| Source situation | Status |
|---|---|
| Official statistic or filing, read at source | verified |
| Company or founder claim about itself | reported |
| Analyst/vendor market estimate, or our derivation | estimate |
| Planning input | assumption |
| Secondary mention we couldn't trace | unverified |
| Conflicts with a stronger source | contradicted |
