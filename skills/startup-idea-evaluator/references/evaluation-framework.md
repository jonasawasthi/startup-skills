# Evaluation framework: question bank (sections A–I)

Answer each question with evidence `[E-…]`, a calculation, or **unknown → validation task**. Questions marked ★ are critical: a weak answer to one of them must appear in the executive evaluation.

## A. Problem
- ★ What exact problem is solved? State it in the customer's words, not the founder's.
- ★ Who experiences it (role, segment, geography)? Is the user the same person as the buyer?
- How often does it occur (daily, weekly, yearly)? Frequency drives retention.
- ★ How painful or expensive is it? Quantify it: hours × wage, money lost, risk.
- What do people do today (the status quo, workarounds, competitors)? What do they pay for it?
- Must-have or nice-to-have? Test: would they use a crappy v1 from an unknown startup? (Graham)
- What evidence of demand exists (behavioural vs opinion)? See [talking to users](../../../knowledge/yc/talking-to-users.md).

## B. Customer
- Ideal customer profile: firmographics or demographics, plus the *trigger* that makes them look for a solution.
- 2–3 personas, separating **user** from **buyer** and **influencer**.
- ★ Early adopters: who has the problem most acutely *and* is reachable *now*?
- Acquisition behaviour: where do they look, and whom do they trust?
- Willingness to pay: evidence (pre-sales, current spend) vs claims.
- Existing alternatives they have tried and abandoned, and why.

## C. Market
- Market definition and scope: geography, year, currency, revenue vs GMV.
- ★ Bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM through the calculator (`startup-kit market-size`).
- Top-down cross-check from dated sources (plausibility only).
- Growth and industry structure (fragmented or concentrated; who holds power).
- Geographic opportunities and expansion sequence (shared payments, language, diaspora, regulation).
- ★ Timing: why now? What changed (technology, regulation, behaviour, cost)?
- Regulatory considerations (payments, data, content, sector licensing).

## D. Competition
- Direct, indirect, substitutes, and the **status quo** (always present).
- Positioning on the 2–4 criteria customers actually use.
- ★ Differentiation: what is 10× better, for whom?
- Defensibility over time ([checklist](../../../knowledge/a16z/defensibility.md)): name the specific type of moat.
- Likely competitive responses if the startup succeeds (incumbent bundling, price cuts, copying).

## E. Business model
- Revenue streams; pricing and packaging; who pays whom.
- Gross margin drivers (payment fees, hosting, support, payouts, human-in-the-loop).
- ★ Unit economics: ARPU, GM, churn, CAC → LTV, payback (calculator). Pre-revenue = assumptions.
- Retention expectations and evidence.
- Monetisation risks: willingness to pay, disintermediation, platform fees, FX.

## F. Product
- Core value proposition in one sentence.
- ★ MVP scope: one user, one job, one metric, timeboxed ([MVP](../../../knowledge/yc/mvp.md)).
- PMF hypotheses (what signal would show fit, for which segment).
- Technical feasibility and key technical risks. If there is a codebase, cite what's *built* vs *planned*.
- Distribution built into the product (loops, invites, creator audiences).
- Network effects (named type) and switching costs; evidence or "unproven".
- Scalability: what breaks at 10× or 100×? Which manual processes exist?

## G. Founder–market fit
- Relevant expertise and track record (only what is provided or public).
- Access to the first 10–100 customers (named channels).
- Unique insight: one sentence that would surprise an informed outsider.
- Execution capability (shipping evidence: git history, launches).
- Advantages and gaps, and how the gaps get filled (hire, advisor, partner).

## H. Risks
Fill the [risk register](../../../knowledge/frameworks/risk-register.md) across market, product, technical, financial, regulatory, operational, distribution and team.

## I. Validation strategy
For each ★ answer that is weak or unknown, design an experiment ([catalogue](../../../knowledge/frameworks/validation-experiments.md)): customer interviews, landing page, pre-sales, concierge MVP, prototype tests, pilots, channel tests. Each needs a **metric, a threshold, a kill criterion, cost, duration and an owner**.
