# Positioning and messaging method

Background: [positioning knowledge](../../../knowledge/gtm/positioning.md).

## 1. Components, in order

| Step | Question | Evidence that counts | If there is none |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive alternatives | What do best-fit customers do today instead? | Interviews, observed workarounds, support tickets, search behaviour | Hypothesis; add to the interview guide |
| Differentiated capabilities | What can we do that those alternatives can't? | Product status `live` or `built` (codebase or product evidence) **and** evidence that alternatives lack it | List as *hypothesised differentiator* |
| Value | What does that capability let the customer achieve? | Customer outcomes, time or money saved, quotes | Phrase as a value hypothesis |
| Best-fit customers | Who cares most about that value? | Behaviour (payments, usage), not demographics alone | ICP hypothesis |
| Market category | Which frame makes the value obvious? | Words customers use; categories they already buy in | Test two framings on the landing page |

## 2. Outputs

1. **Positioning statement** (an internal record; Moore's structure): *For* <best-fit customer> *who* <trigger or need>, <product> *is a* <category> *that* <key value>. *Unlike* <primary alternative>, *it* <differentiated capability>.
2. **Elevator pitch** (≤ 40 words): problem → who → what changes → why us.
3. **Homepage messaging**: headline (value, ≤ 10 words), subhead (who and how), three proof-backed benefits, a social-proof slot (empty until real), the primary CTA.
4. **Landing-page structure**: hero → problem in customer words → how it works (3 steps) → benefits with proof → objections or FAQ → pricing or offer → CTA. One page per segment when segments differ in pain.
5. **Feature → benefit → proof**:

   | Feature (status) | Benefit (customer words) | Proof (evidence ID or *hypothesis*) |
   |---|---|---|

6. **Objection handling**: objection → underlying fear → response → proof. Draw objections from interviews and lost deals. Before launch, label them *anticipated*.
7. **Segment messaging**: a row per segment with pain, promise, proof, channel and CTA. Supply and demand on a marketplace are separate segments.

## Rules

- No superlatives without proof ("the best", "the only", "the first").
- Never write social proof (testimonials, logos, user counts) that doesn't exist. Leave the slot and add it to the missing information list.
- Messaging uses the customer's words from interviews. Quote those interviews in the ledger as `interview` evidence.
- One positioning, many messages. If two segments need different positioning, they are two products or two GTM motions. Say so.
