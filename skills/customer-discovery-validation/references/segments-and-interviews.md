# Segments and interview planning

Background: [segmentation and early adopters](../../../knowledge/customer-discovery/segmentation-and-early-adopters.md), [interview methodology](../../../knowledge/customer-discovery/interview-methodology.md), [jobs to be done](../../../knowledge/customer-discovery/jobs-to-be-done.md).

## Segment designer

For each segment, record:

| Field | B2C | B2B | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Demographics plus circumstance (life stage, trigger) | Firmographics (size, sector, tools) plus role | One segment per side; never mix supply and demand |
| Roles | User and buyer often the same (check: parent, partner, employer) | User ≠ buyer ≠ decision-maker; list influencers | Supply and demand each have their own roles |
| Jobs | Job statement in the customer's words | Job of the role, and of the organisation | Supply: earn, reach, operate. Demand: find, trust, buy |
| Early-adopter signals | Workaround, active search, recent spend | Spreadsheet process, a job post, budget line | Supply: already sells informally. Demand: already pays informally |
| Priority | Pain, access, WTP, reachable size (1–5, with a reason) | Same | Usually start with the side that is harder to recruit and brings the other side |

Score priority to decide **whom to interview first**. It is not a market size.

## Interview planning

`startup-kit discovery interviews <spec>` generates one guide per segment. It contains the research objectives, the hypotheses it informs (never shown to the participant), participant criteria, recruitment, a 5-part script (context → problem story → alternatives → spending and decisions → close with a commitment ask), follow-up prompts, and things to avoid. Tailor the guide to the segment:

- **B2B:** add "walk me through how a new tool was last approved", and interview buyers and users separately.
- **B2C:** ask about the last purchase in the category and who influenced it.
- **Supply side of a marketplace:** ask how they find customers today, what they earn, what they pay intermediaries, and how they get paid.
- **Demand side:** ask how they found the last provider, what they paid, and what made them trust that provider.

**Lint every edited guide** with `startup-kit discovery check guide.md`. It flags:

- hypothetical questions ("would you…", "if we built…");
- leading questions ("don't you think…");
- pitching ("our app…");
- compound questions;
- closed yes/no questions (as info).

It also warns when fewer than 40% of the questions ask about actual past behaviour.

## Recruitment

- Recruit from observable signals: communities, workarounds, reviews of competitors, and public complaints.
- Avoid friends and family, or label their interviews as such.
- Record consent, use pseudonymous codes, and say in advance that this isn't a sales call.
