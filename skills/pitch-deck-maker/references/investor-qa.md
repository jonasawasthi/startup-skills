# Investor Q&A bank

Write 15–30 questions tailored to the company. For each one, give an **honest suggested answer**, the **evidence** that supports it, and a **gap note** if the answer is weak.

## Questions investors commonly ask (by theme)

**Problem and customer**: Who exactly is the customer? Who pays? How do you know they have this problem? What do they use today?

**Product**: What's live versus planned? What do users do in the first session? What's retention like? Why will they keep using it?

**Market**: How did you size the market? What's the beachhead? Why is this a venture-scale outcome?

**Why now**: What changed? Why hasn't an incumbent done this?

**Competition**: Who else does this? What happens if [incumbent] copies you? What's your moat in 3 years?

**Business model**: How do you make money? What are the margins after payment and payout fees? What's the CAC, and from which channel?

**Traction**: How is "active" defined? What's the growth rate, and from what base? Is growth organic or paid?

**Team**: Why you? What's missing on the team? Is everyone full-time? How is equity split?

**Go-to-market**: How do you get the first 100 customers? The next 1,000?

**Financials and the raise**: How much are you raising, and why that amount? What milestones does it reach? What's the runway? Are you default alive?

**Risks**: What could kill the company? What regulatory exposure do you have (payments, data, content)?

**Ecosystem companies**: Why build several products at once? Which one matters most? What do you stop doing if resources get tight?

## Answer principles

- Lead with the direct answer, then the evidence.
- When the honest answer is "we don't know yet", say so and name the experiment and its date.
- Never contradict the deck. If the deck is wrong, fix the deck.
